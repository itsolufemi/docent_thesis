So, this one is actually quite a rare treat because Landseer usually stuck to his classic animal scenes, but here he's diving into "Orientalism," which was a big fashion trend back then. The point of the painting is really to showcase a luxurious, exotic atmosphere. He tells this story by layering the animals—you've got the mare and foal at the bottom, then some greyhounds, and finally monkeys at the top. I'd read that as a bit of a visual ladder, where the animals get "more intelligent" as you look higher up the canvas.

Well, if you look over on the right, you'll see a jar with two pipes sticking out of it. There aren't any people in the scene, but I'd say those pipes are Landseer's way of telling us the owner is just nearby. 

It's also worth mentioning that this was a massive purchase for Sir Richard Wallace. He bought it from the Prince of Wales for over six thousand pounds, which was a huge sum back then—I think the Prince might've been a bit short of cash and took advantage of Wallace's generosity.

Well, the "social meaning" here is actually quite interesting because it's split between what's inside the frame and the history of the painting itself. Inside, Landseer's playing with a sort of intellectual hierarchy; if you look at the animals, the ones higher up in the composition—like the monkeys—are the most intelligent. 

But then there's the social story of the painting's ownership. Sir Richard Wallace bought it from the Prince of Wales for a huge sum of money, and I'd read that as Wallace perhaps trying to buy his way into a higher social circle, while the Prince, who was always short on cash, likely took advantage of that.

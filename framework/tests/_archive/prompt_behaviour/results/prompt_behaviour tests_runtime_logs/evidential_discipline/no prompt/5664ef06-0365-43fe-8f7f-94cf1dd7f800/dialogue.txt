Well, this one's really about a hierarchy of intelligence. Landseer's basically telling a story through the positioning of the animals: the further up the painting you look, the "smarter" the animals get, moving from the mare and foal at the bottom up to the monkeys at the top. It's a bit of a playful take on the "Orientalist" style that was really trendy back in the Victorian era.

Well, it's an interpretation based on how the animals are arranged, rather than something Landseer explicitly wrote down. I mean, looking at the composition, the progression from the horse and foal up to those monkeys—one even has an earring—really suggests a climb up a ladder of intelligence. It's more of a visual clue than a stated fact, but that's often how these Victorian paintings worked.

Well, for one, it's a really interesting look at 'Orientalism', which was a huge trend back then—essentially the Victorian fascination with the Middle East. Landseer tells this story by focusing on a hierarchy of animals; if you look from the bottom up, the creatures actually get more "intelligent," ending with those monkeys at the top. Oh, and it's also a bit of a legend in the collection because Sir Richard Wallace paid a small fortune for it, possibly just to impress the Prince of Wales.

Right, I see what you mean. You're after the artistic side of things rather than just the history. 

Well, look at how Landseer handles the composition. He creates this clever little puzzle where the "intelligence" of the animals increases as your eye moves up the canvas, from the mare and foal at the bottom up to those monkeys at the top. It's a really playful way of organizing the scene, and it makes you look closer at the details—like the earring on one of the monkeys—to see how he's playing with that idea.

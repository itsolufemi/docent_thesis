Well, this one's actually quite a bit of a curiosity because Landseer usually didn't do "Orientalist" themes—he's much more famous for his Highland cattle and dogs. The painting is really a study of hierarchy and intelligence; if you look at the animals, the "smarter" they are, the higher up in the frame they're placed, going from the horses at the bottom up to those monkeys at the top. It's also a bit of a status symbol for the collection, as Sir Richard Wallace paid a massive sum for it, likely to get into the good graces of the Prince of Wales.

Right, sorry about that! So, the reason it's relevant here is that it's actually a bit of a rarity for Landseer. He was the absolute superstar of the Victorian era, but he almost always stuck to British landscapes and animals. For him to dive into these exotic, Middle Eastern themes was a bit of a departure—he was basically tapping into a trend that was very fashionable for wealthy collectors at the time.

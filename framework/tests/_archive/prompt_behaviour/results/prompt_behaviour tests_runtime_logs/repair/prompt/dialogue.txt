Well, the main thing to know is that this was a huge deal in the Victorian art world—it's actually one of the most expensive paintings ever bought by the founders of this collection. Landseer was the "it" painter of the time, and this piece is a bit of a rarity for him because he's playing with these fashionable "Orientalist" themes that were all the rage back then.

If you look closely, there's a clever little hierarchy going on: as your eye moves up the painting, the animals actually get more "intelligent," going from the horses to the greyhounds and finally to the monkeys at the top.

Oh, sorry about that! I should've explained. Basically, "Orientalism" was a huge trend in 19th-century European art and literature. It was all about the West's fascination with the "East"—mostly the Middle East and Asia—often depicting these places as exotic, mysterious, or sort of romanticised. 

In this painting, Landseer uses things like the tent, the specific breed of horses, and those monkeys to give it that "foreign" and luxury feel that Victorian collectors were obsessed with.

Well, for starters, it's a bit of a rarity for Landseer. He was the most fashionable painter of the Victorian era, but he didn't usually do "Orientalist" themes like this—which is why it's so interesting to see him tackle an Arab scene. 

The painting is really a study of animal intelligence; if you look, the animals actually get "smarter" the higher up the canvas you go, from the mare and foal at the bottom up to the monkeys at the top. Oh, and it's also a bit of a trophy piece—Sir Richard Wallace paid a huge sum for it, likely because it came from the Prince of Wales.

Right, I get what you mean. Looking at it just as a piece of art, it's all about how Landseer uses these animals to tell a story without any people actually being in the frame. He's basically creating a puzzle for us; you've got the mare and foal, then the greyhounds, and finally those monkeys—one even has an earring—which suggests a very specific, wealthy human presence. It's a clever way of showing a luxurious lifestyle and a bit of exoticism just through the "pets" and the props, like those pipes in the jar.

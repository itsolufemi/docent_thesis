Well, basically Landseer is playing with the idea of intelligence here. He's told a little story through the animals: if you look at the painting from bottom to top, the animals actually get more "intelligent," starting with the mare and foal, moving up to the greyhounds, and ending with those monkeys—one of whom is even wearing an earring. It's a bit of a nod to the "Orientalist" fashion of the time, creating this exotic, curated scene.

Well, that's a fair point. If we look at it from a different angle, it's less about a formal statement of rank and more about the *vibe* of the person. Lawrence focuses so much on her face and that particular expression, while the dress is actually pretty simple. I mean, he's really capturing her personal charisma and the "voluptuousness" that had all of London talking at the time, rather than just listing her titles.
